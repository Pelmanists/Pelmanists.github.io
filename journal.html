Weekly updates on what we did, what we learned, and what's next. Newest entries first.

  1. Weeks 5 to 9, Oct 19 to Nov 20

    GP3: building Helper

    We turned our GP2 paper workflow into Helper, a working conversational assistant for adults over 70. Helper reminds people to take their pills and shows a simulated caregiver alert when a dose is missed. It also plays a memory game with sample family photos and walks people through phone tasks one step at a time. Once a free Rasa licence is added to .env, it starts in Docker with one script. Full write-up on the GP3 page.

    What we did

    • Built the dialogue in Rasa CALM: medication reminders with a repeat-back check, listing and deleting reminders, pill-time check-ins that record whether the dose was taken, a photo memory game with hints, and step-by-step phone help
    • Built a browser interface for phone, tablet, or desktop, with large buttons, bigger text, high contrast, read-aloud, repeat, and speech-to-text
    • Packaged Helper as two Docker Compose services, nginx and Rasa Pro, with reminders saved in SQLite on a Docker volume, and wrote start and stop scripts
    • Wrote a technical README covering setup, a 5-minute test, troubleshooting, privacy, and known limitations

    What we learned

    • Letting the language model interpret requests while Helper's replies stay fixed means medicine names and times are always repeated back exactly, which supports the predictability we identified in GP2.
    • Buttons have to work on their own: if the hosted model is down or misreads a request, the button paths still work.
    • Privacy needs care: relevant data, like medicine names, is sent to the hosted model, so our privacy notes must say so plainly.

    Next week

    • Start GP4: put Helper on a public live deployment and test it with users
  2. Weeks 3 to 4, Oct 5 to 16

    GP2: from needfinding to a paper workflow

    We started Project 2, a conversational AI assistant for older adults. We each interviewed an older adult over 70 about technology and AI, narrowed our problem to medication reminders, wrote two personas, and drew the assistant's conversation on paper. Full write-up on the GP2 page.

    What we did

    • Each interviewed an older adult over 70 about everyday technology and attitudes toward AI; Ethan summarized two of the interviews
    • Wrote a problem statement focused on medication reminders, with a memory game and phone help kept for later
    • Created two personas: Techbite Bill, who is very comfortable with technology, and Outdated Dan, who has no experience with it
    • Drew a paper flowchart of the conversation, with fallbacks and points where a caregiver or family member steps in

    What we learned

    • Comfort with technology varies widely: one participant needs slow, step-by-step help, while the other builds websites and already uses AI as a backup for his memory.
    • Updates that change things, autocorrect mistakes, and the feeling that personal data is being sold all erode trust, so the assistant should confirm what it heard and stay predictable.

    Next week

    • Start GP3: build the conversational assistant
  3. Week 2, Sep 28 to Oct 2

    GP1: from interview to headset

    We each interviewed an older adult about memory and daily routines, then reconvened to find a direction for our project. From there we prototyped a 9-screen memory activity on paper, built it in ForgeXR, and played it on the Meta Quest 3. Full write-up on the GP1 page.

    What we did

    • We all interviewed an older adult about memory and daily routines, then reconvened to find a direction for our project
    • Ran the whole activity with paper cards before touching the authoring tool
    • Built a 9-screen ForgeXR lesson: watch and repeat a color pattern, match colored shapes to numbers, and put the steps of making tea in order
    • Recorded a playthrough of all nine screens in the headset, then fixed the problems we found in a revised version

    What we learned

    • Memory trouble shows up most in everyday routines, so practice should be built around familiar tasks like making tea.
    • Our participants wished some kind of help existed, which pushed us toward a calm, supportive design with no timers or penalties.

    • Start GP2: needfinding, problem definition, and personas
